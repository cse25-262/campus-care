Common cold

  • Rest and drink plenty of fluids
  • Warm drinks, saline nose spray or steam can ease congestion
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for aches, following the label
Get help if: symptoms last longer than 10 days, or you develop a high fever or breathing difficulty.

Influenza (flu)

  • Rest, fluids and fever relief
  • Stay away from classes and shared spaces while feverish
  • Cover coughs and wash hands often
Get help if: you have asthma or another long-term condition, symptoms worsen after a few days, or you struggle to breathe.

Tension headache

  • Drink water and eat regular meals
  • Take screen breaks and stretch your neck and shoulders
  • Prioritise 7 to 9 hours of sleep
Get help if: the headache is sudden and severe, follows a head injury, or comes with fever and a stiff neck.

Migraine

  • Rest in a dark, quiet room
  • Keep a diary of triggers such as skipped meals, poor sleep or stress
  • A pharmacist can advise on suitable pain relief
Get help if: migraines are frequent, new for you, or come with weakness, confusion or vision loss.

Sore throat (usually viral)

  • Warm fluids, lozenges and rest
  • Gargle with warm salty water
  • Avoid smoking and vaping
Get help if: you cannot swallow, have a very high fever, or symptoms last more than a week.

Gastroenteritis (stomach bug)

  • Sip oral rehydration solution or water often
  • Eat bland food once vomiting settles
  • Wash hands thoroughly; avoid preparing food for others
Get help if: there is blood in stool, signs of dehydration (very dark urine, dizziness), or it lasts over 3 days.

Indigestion or reflux

  • Eat smaller meals and avoid late-night eating
  • Limit caffeine, alcohol and very spicy food
  • A pharmacist can suggest antacids
Get help if: pain is severe, persistent, or you vomit blood or lose weight without trying.

Stress or sleep deprivation

  • Keep a regular sleep schedule and limit late caffeine
  • Break study into blocks with short walks
  • Talk to a friend, mentor or counsellor early
Get help if: symptoms affect your studies or daily life for more than two weeks.

Anxiety

  • Try slow breathing: in for 4 counts, out for 6
  • Move your body daily and limit caffeine
  • Counselling and talking therapies are effective
Get help if: anxiety is constant, you have panic attacks, or it limits what you can do.

Low mood or depression

  • Keep simple routines: sleep, meals, daylight, movement
  • Tell someone you trust how you are feeling
  • Student counselling services are there for this
Get help if: low mood lasts more than two weeks. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, seek help immediately.

Urinary tract infection

  • Drink water and avoid holding urine
  • A clinician can confirm and prescribe treatment if needed
Get help if: you have fever, back or side pain, or blood in urine. Get seen the same day.

Muscle strain

  • Rest, then gentle movement
  • Apply a cold pack in the first 48 hours, then warmth
  • Check your desk and study posture
Get help if: pain follows a fall, causes numbness, or has not improved after two weeks.

Hay fever or allergic rhinitis

  • Avoid known triggers and keep windows closed on high-pollen days
  • Shower after being outdoors
  • Ask a pharmacist about antihistamines
Get help if: symptoms disrupt sleep or you also wheeze or feel breathless.

Conjunctivitis (pink eye)

  • Clean eyelids with clean, cool water and a fresh cloth
  • Do not share towels; wash hands often
  • Stop wearing contact lenses until it clears
Get help if: pain, light sensitivity, reduced vision, or no improvement after a few days.

Skin irritation or contact dermatitis

  • Avoid the suspected trigger such as a new soap or detergent
  • Use fragrance-free moisturiser
  • A pharmacist can suggest mild creams
Get help if: the rash spreads quickly, blisters, looks infected, or comes with fever.

Asthma or lung problem

  • Sit upright and use your prescribed inhaler if you have one
  • Move away from smoke or allergens
Get help if: breathlessness is new, severe, or not easing. Seek urgent care.

Possible heart or serious chest problem

  • Stop what you are doing and call emergency services
  • Do not drive yourself
Get help if: chest pain or pressure with breathlessness needs emergency assessment immediately.

These are possibilities based on common patterns, not a diagnosis. Many conditions share symptoms.

Health topics

Short, practical guides on what affects students most.

Sleep and study

Most adults need 7 to 9 hours. Keep a steady wake time, avoid caffeine after midday, and keep screens out of bed. All-nighters hurt memory more than they help.

Managing stress

Plan your week, break big tasks down, and move daily. Stress that disrupts sleep or appetite for weeks deserves support from counselling.

Anxiety and low mood

Common among students and very treatable. Talking therapies, routine, exercise and social connection all help. Reach out early.

Eating well on a budget

Build meals around beans, eggs, vegetables, whole grains and fruit. Don’t skip breakfast, and keep water with you.

Staying active

Aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly. Walking between classes and short stretch breaks count.

Infection prevention

Wash hands, stay up to date with vaccinations, and don’t share cups or utensils. Stay home when you have a fever.

Sexual and reproductive health

Use condoms, get tested regularly, and ask a clinic about contraception. Services are confidential and judgement-free.

Alcohol, vaping and drugs

Know the risks, never mix substances, look after friends, and ask for help if use feels out of control.

Eyes, posture and screens

Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes look 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Set your screen at eye level.

First aid basics

Cool burns under running water for 20 minutes, apply pressure to bleeding, and call emergency services for serious injuries.

Health services for students

Know what each service is for and what to bring. Check your own campus website or student affairs office for exact locations, hours and costs.

Campus health clinic

First stop for illness, injuries, check-ups, vaccinations and referrals.

Bring: student ID, medical history, list of current medicines.

Counselling service

Confidential support for stress, anxiety, low mood, grief and relationships. Often free for students.

Ask: how to book, wait times, and whether crisis appointments exist.

Pharmacy

Advice and treatment for minor ailments such as colds, hay fever, mild pain and skin irritation.

Ask: whether the medicine is safe with others you take.

Sexual and reproductive health clinic

Contraception, STI testing, pregnancy care and counselling, confidential.

Ask: what testing is available and how results are shared.

Emergency department

For chest pain, severe breathing trouble, serious injury, heavy bleeding or loss of consciousness.

Call your local emergency number if you can’t travel safely.

Crisis and helplines

Free, confidential support if you feel overwhelmed or unsafe, available outside office hours.

Search your country’s official mental health helpline.
